## A. Expert Summary

University Hill Farms — locally shortened to "Hill Farms" — is an established, built-out mid-century neighborhood on Madison's near west side, not a new-construction subdivision. It was developed starting in the 1950s when the University of Wisconsin subdivided roughly 487–600 acres of a former experimental farm (sources give both figures — see flag below) into a planned, self-contained suburb, in partnership with the City of Madison. Part of the neighborhood — the University Hill Farms Historic District — was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 2015 for its mid-century Modern Movement architecture (Ranch and Contemporary styles, including one Frank Lloyd Wright–designed house) and for its significance as a planned community. This is fundamentally different from the newer-construction Dane County subdivisions in this project's other cut sheets: there are no active phases, no builder, and no lots for sale — it's resale housing in a 70-year-old neighborhood. The name has no meaningful collision risk with other "University Hill Farms" developments nationally in the sources found; every result returned was specific to Madison, WI. Do not confuse it with the separate "Hill Farms State Office Building" redevelopment site (a large mixed-use state/private project on the neighborhood's north edge) — that's a government office/mixed-use project embedded near the neighborhood's boundary, not the residential neighborhood itself, though it sits inside/adjacent to the Hill Farms boundary and is directly relevant to nearby-amenities questions (Section H/I).

## C. Development and Phase History

- Land origin: part of a University of Wisconsin experimental/agricultural research farm.
- **1953**: Wisconsin state legislature authorized sale of the UW farmland for development (single-source, homes.com/Public School Review aggregator — not independently corroborated).
- **Starting in the 1950s**: UW and the City of Madison collaborated to subdivide the farm into a planned, self-contained suburban community — intended to include housing, schools, parks, churches, shopping, and offices in one walkable neighborhood, per the aggregator summary.
- Homes built are predominantly mid-century: Ranch, split-level, Contemporary, bi-level, and some Colonial Revival styles, largely dating to the 1950s–1960s build-out era (exact construction date range not independently confirmed for the neighborhood as a whole; the National Register nomination would have this, but the primary NR document (wisconsinhistory.org NR2471) was not directly read in this pass).
- **2015**: A defined "University Hill Farms Historic District" (a subset of the broader neighborhood, not necessarily every parcel within the association's boundaries) was added to the National Register of Historic Places.
- **Gap:** This is a fully built-out, decades-old neighborhood — there is no active "phase" construction to report the way there would be for a new subdivision. No source found gives an exact build-out completion year.
- **Not confident**: the precise boundary of the Historic District vs. the boundary of the broader "University Hill Farms" neighborhood/association area — these are likely not identical (historic districts are often a subset), and this research pass did not confirm whether they match. John should treat "Historic District" and "neighborhood" as related but not necessarily coextensive until verified.

## E. Schools (District + Assignments — Verify by Address)

- **District:** Madison Metropolitan School District (MMSD) — confirmed.
- **Reported elementary schools serving the neighborhood:** Van Hise Elementary and Glenn Stephens (Stephens) Elementary — both named consistently across hillfarms.org and homes.com summaries.
- **Middle school:** Hamilton Middle School.
- **High school:** Madison West High School, 30 Ash St, Madison, WI 53726 — "all property in Hill Farms is zoned to Madison West High School" per one aggregator summary.
- **School ratings mentioned (Niche, via aggregator):** Van Hise Elementary ranked #10 among Madison-area public elementary schools; Hamilton Middle ranked #5 among Madison-area public middle schools; West High ranked #3 among Madison-area public high schools; all three given an overall "A" Niche grade. **This is a single-source (aggregator) ranking claim — not independently corroborated, and Niche rankings/grades change over time. Treat as dated/for-context only, not to be quoted as current fact without checking niche.com directly.**
- **CAVEAT — do not guarantee:** A search on MMSD's own attendance-area documentation surfaced a specific, important detail: **Stephens Elementary is noted as having "island zones" in its attendance area on Madison's west side** — meaning Stephens' boundary is not a simple contiguous shape, and parcels can be assigned to Stephens or another elementary school in a non-obvious, address-specific pattern. This makes it especially important that John (or the buyer) run any specific University Hill Farms address through MMSD's official "Find Your School" tool or the interactive attendance-area map at madison.k12.wi.us/about/attendance-areas before stating an elementary assignment. **Do not assume every Hill Farms address feeds Van Hise or every address feeds Stephens** — within this neighborhood, the two elementary zones likely split the area, and which side of which street a home sits on matters.

## G. HOA and Restrictions

- The **University Hill Farms Neighborhood Association** is explicitly described as "an optional cooperative among neighborhood residents" — **not a mandatory HOA governing architectural review, assessments, or deed-recorded restrictions** in the way a typical subdivision HOA would. Membership requires only an annual fee (reported inconsistently as $10 vs. $20 — see Section B flag) and residing within the defined boundary.
- **No source found describing deed restrictions, architectural control covenants, or mandatory assessments** for the neighborhood as a whole. Given the age of the neighborhood (1950s platting) there may be legacy deed restrictions on individual parcels/subdivisions-within-the-neighborhood, but this pass found no documentation of them.
- **Separately, the Hill Farm Swim Club is its own private membership organization** (stock-share model: reported $700 stock purchase + annual dues + a separate swim/dive team fee) — this is optional, pay-to-join amenity access, not a neighborhood-wide HOA fee, and should never be described as a blanket cost of living in Hill Farms. **Do not apply the swim club's fee structure to the neighborhood association, or vice versa** — these are two separate organizations with two separate fee structures, both found in search results and easy to conflate.
- **Not confident** on current-year dollar figures for either the association dues or swim club dues/stock price — both should be verified directly (hillfarms.org/membership and hillfarmswimclub.org) before quoting numbers to a client.

## J. What Residents Value

## K. Possible Considerations

- **Major nearby redevelopment activity**: the ~21-acre, ~$196M Madison Yards at Hill Farms project sits directly on the neighborhood's boundary (Segoe/Sheboygan corner) and involves years of construction, increased traffic, and a large new state office building with associated employee population and parking garage. Buyers should be told this is an active, multi-year redevelopment adjacent to the neighborhood, not a stable/unchanging edge.
- **This is an older housing stock** (1950s-60s construction) — buyers should expect original-era systems/materials in unrenovated homes (common inspection considerations: aging electrical, plumbing, insulation, roofs) even though this wasn't directly documented in sourced material; this is a general inference from the construction era and should be flagged as such, not stated as confirmed fact about any specific home.
- **Elementary school boundary complexity** (Stephens' "island zones") means two nearby-looking homes in the same neighborhood could be assigned to different elementary schools — a genuine consideration for buyers who care about a specific school, not just the district.
- **Two easily-conflated fee-charging organizations** (neighborhood association vs. swim club) with different purposes and different, unverified-in-this-pass dollar amounts — risk of misquoting one as the other.
- **Historic District status** (2015 National Register listing) may carry implications for exterior renovation/permitting on the subset of homes actually within the historic district boundary — this pass did not confirm whether NR listing imposes any binding local design-review requirement (National Register listing itself is typically honorific/tax-incentive-related and does not by itself restrict private owners absent a local historic-preservation ordinance overlay — but whether Madison has layered a local landmark/conservation district on top of this was not confirmed). **Flag for verification** before telling a buyer whether exterior changes face added scrutiny.

## M. Frequently Asked Questions

**Is University Hill Farms a new subdivision?**
No — it's an established neighborhood built out mostly in the 1950s-60s on former University of Wisconsin farmland. There is no active builder or new-construction phase.

**Is there an HOA?**
There's a voluntary neighborhood association (not a mandatory recorded HOA) with a modest annual membership fee — **exact current fee not confirmed in this pass, verify before quoting** (see Section G).

**What schools does University Hill Farms feed into?**
Reported as Van Hise or Glenn Stephens Elementary, Hamilton Middle School, and Madison West High School — all MMSD. **Elementary assignment is address-specific and should always be verified**, especially given Stephens Elementary's reported "island zone" boundary pattern.

**Is there a neighborhood pool?**
There's a private membership swim club (Hill Farm Swim Club) — not a free/included amenity, and not the same organization as the neighborhood association.

**What's the big construction project near the neighborhood?**
The "Madison Yards at Hill Farms" redevelopment of the former Hill Farms State Office Building site at Segoe Rd/Sheboygan Ave — a large mixed-use project; the new state office tower and a Whole Foods appear to be open, but the full original mixed-use scope should not be presented as fully completed without current verification.

**Is this the same as any "University Hill Farms" subdivision elsewhere in the US?**
No collision found in this research pass — every source returned was specific to Madison, WI. See Discarded section in Part 2 file for the collision-check methodology.
