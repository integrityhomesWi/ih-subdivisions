# Maple Bluff — Madison, WI

**IMPORTANT STRUCTURAL FLAG — READ FIRST:** "Maple Bluff" is not a subdivision or plat inside the City of Madison. It is the **independent, incorporated Village of Maple Bluff**, a separate municipality in Dane County that borders the City of Madison on three sides and Lake Mendota on the fourth. It has its own village government, police department, zoning code, and tax roll — it is not annexed to Madison and never has been (residents petitioned for annexation in 1929, were rejected/withdrew, and incorporated independently instead). Any brief, ad copy, or listing content for John should say "Village of Maple Bluff" or "the Village of Maple Bluff, adjacent to Madison" rather than implying it's a Madison neighborhood/subdivision. This distinction matters for tax bills, school-district framing (it happens to still be in MMSD, see Section E), and legal accuracy in marketing copy.

## A. Expert Summary

Maple Bluff is a small (0.69–0.70 sq mi), affluent, lakefront village on the eastern shore of Lake Mendota, immediately northeast of downtown Madison and directly north of the Sherman Avenue corridor. Incorporated in 1929 (originally as the "Village of Lakewood Bluff"), it was platted starting around 1915 by the Lakewood Land Company on land tied to Leonard Farwell's 1840s land holdings. It is one of the most exclusive residential addresses in the Madison metro, known for large lots, mature maple tree canopy, lake and bluff views, the private Maple Bluff Country Club (founded 1899), a public village beach park, and as the site of the Wisconsin Governor's Mansion. Population is small (~1,368 at 2020 census). Homes are architecturally distinct (1920s–1950s Colonial Revival, Cape Cod, Tudor Revival, plus midcentury ranches and some newer infill/rebuilds). Not confident on current total housing unit count — flag for MLS/Village verification.

## C. Development and Phase History

- Land originally associated with Leonard Farwell, who arrived in Madison in 1847 and acquired large tracts on the city's east side.
- Farwell Drive, the first permanent road through what is now Maple Bluff, was completed in 1897 by the Madison Park and Pleasure Drive Association.
- Maple Bluff Country Club founded 1899 — predates the residential village itself.
- By 1915, the Lakewood Land Company had platted most of present-day Maple Bluff. Madison city planner John Nolen originally proposed a denser plat (smaller lots, more streets) for the "Lakewood"/Lower Bluff section; the Lakewood Land Company rejected that plan in favor of the more spacious layout that exists today.
- Incorporated as the Village of Lakewood Bluff on May 17, 1929, after residents' 1929 petition to annex into the City of Madison was not pursued to completion — driven at the time by inadequate rural infrastructure/services. (Not confident on the exact renaming date to "Maple Bluff" from "Lakewood Bluff" — flag for verification.)
- Housing stock reflects mostly early-to-mid 20th century construction (1920s–1950s) with later infill: midcentury modern ranches and some early-2000s and newer builds/rebuilds mentioned by real estate sources. This is not phase-by-phase subdivision data (there is no "Phase 1/Phase 2" plat structure like a modern platted subdivision) — Maple Bluff developed organically over a century, not in discrete builder phases.

## G. HOA and Restrictions

There is **no traditional homeowners' association** in the conventional subdivision-HOA sense — Maple Bluff is governed as its own municipality with a Village Board, its own police department, and its own zoning code (Chapter 225 of the Village's Municipal Code), rather than by a private HOA with CC&Rs. Key regulatory points confirmed:

- Zoning ordinance: Chapter 225, comprehensively updated and adopted by the Village Board on **June 12, 2025**. Divides the village into zoning districts with district-specific rules on land use, setbacks, building design, and signage.
- Floodplain zoning regulations/maps were updated per WDNR/FEMA requirements (specific adoption date not confirmed in this pass — flag).
- Short-term rental restriction (confirmed): a single-family residence **may not be rented for 6 or fewer days**. Rentals of more than 6 but fewer than 30 consecutive days to a single family are classified as a "medium-term rental residence" and require the operator to obtain a Village license.
- Village residents get preferential access to Maple Bluff Country Club membership (half the standard initiation fee, and new residents can bypass the golf waiting list if they apply within 30 days of establishing residency) — this is a club-membership perk, not a legal HOA obligation; MBCC membership itself is optional/private, not mandatory for residents.

**Flag:** Any specific setback, height, or accessory-structure rule referenced for a client should be pulled from the live Chapter 225 zoning ordinance text (villageofmaplebluff.com / eCode360), not summarized secondhand, since this is legally consequential content.

## H. Parks, Trails, and Amenities

- Village park system: one source (Islands.com/Islands travel-content site) states the Village maintains **six parks**, including **Beach Park** (lakefront swim beach, described as a popular sunset spot, with tennis courts nearby). This "six parks" figure is from a single travel-content source and was not independently cross-verified against the Village's own parks page (blocked from direct fetch) — flag as not fully confirmed.
- Maple Bluff Country Club (private, membership-based): 18-hole golf course founded 1899, tennis, swimming pool, Italian Renaissance-style clubhouse. This is a **private club**, not a public village amenity — do not present it as open to the general public.
- Adjacent (just outside the village, in the City of Madison): Warner Park, at 1101 Woodward Drive, with a public beach, boat launch (1201 Woodward Drive, permit required), off-leash dog park, Frautschi Family Accessible Playground, and the Warner Park Community Recreation Center. Warner Park is described as bordering/near the north edge of Maple Bluff. This is a **City of Madison park**, not a Maple Bluff village park — keep that distinction clear in copy.
- Community/social events referenced by sources: community concerts, movies at Beach Park, an annual bocce ball fundraiser, holiday parties, and annual races — these appear to be recurring Village/community events but were not confirmed against an official Village calendar in this pass.

No. It is a separate, independently incorporated village in Dane County, bordered by Madison on three sides and Lake Mendota on the fourth. (Confirmed.)

**What school district serves Maple Bluff?**
Madison Metropolitan School District (MMSD). (Confirmed at the district level; specific school assignment must be verified by address via MMSD's attendance-area tool — not confirmed here.)

**Is there an HOA in Maple Bluff?**
Not in the private-HOA sense. The Village itself functions as the governing/regulatory body via its Village Board and zoning code (Chapter 225), rather than a homeowners' association with CC&Rs. (Confirmed structure; not confident there is zero private-association overlay for any specific sub-area — flag.)

**Can I rent my house out short-term (Airbnb-style) in Maple Bluff?**
Rentals of 6 or fewer days are not permitted for a single-family residence. Rentals of more than 6 but fewer than 30 days require a Village "medium-term rental residence" license. (Confirmed per Village zoning-related search results; verify current ordinance text before advising a client.)

**Is Maple Bluff Country Club open to the public?**
No, it's a private club. Village residents get preferential membership terms (reduced initiation fee, expedited access off the golf waiting list) but membership itself is a separate, optional cost. (Confirmed.)

**How big is Maple Bluff?**
About 0.7 square miles, population approximately 1,368 as of the 2020 Census. (Confirmed for that census year; not confident on any more current population estimate.)

## N. "Only a Local Would Know" Content Ideas (10+)

1. Maple Bluff is legally its own town, not a Madison "neighborhood" — most out-of-town buyers don't realize it has its own police department and village government.
2. It was originally incorporated in 1929 as the "Village of Lakewood Bluff," not "Maple Bluff" — a naming detail worth a quick historical aside in content (verify exact rename date before publishing).
3. The Wisconsin Governor's Mansion sits inside Maple Bluff — a fact many Madison-area residents don't know, let alone out-of-towners.
4. The dense, small-lot plat you'd expect from a 1915-era streetcar suburb almost happened here — city planner John Nolen's original denser plan for the "Lakewood"/Lower Bluff section was rejected by the Lakewood Land Company in favor of today's more spacious layout. That's why lots feel bigger than typical 1920s-era subdivisions.
5. Maple Bluff Country Club (1899) actually predates the residential village itself by three decades.
6. Residents get a "locals' discount" and jump-the-line privilege on Maple Bluff Country Club membership — a real, quantifiable perk worth mentioning to relocation buyers (verify current terms with MBCC before quoting numbers).
7. The village's western boundary is the Lake Mendota shoreline itself — sunset-facing lake views are a genuine differentiator versus most Madison-area subdivisions that don't touch open water.
8. Its proximity to Dane County Regional Airport (roughly a few miles, see Part 2) is unusually close for a high-end residential area — a selling point for frequent flyers, but also worth flagging for noise-sensitive buyers.
9. Warner Park — with its public beach, boat launch, dog park, and Madison Mallards baseball — sits just outside Maple Bluff's boundary and functions as the "public" recreational counterpart to the private MBCC.
10. Short-term rental rules are genuinely restrictive here (no rentals under 6 days) — this is not an Airbnb-friendly village, unlike some lake-adjacent communities elsewhere in the county.
11. The Robert M. La Follette House (a nationally significant political-history site) is located in Maple Bluff, not downtown Madison as some might assume.
12. The village underwent a full zoning ordinance rewrite adopted June 12, 2025 — current buyers/builders are operating under a genuinely new code, not the older one referenced in older articles.
