## A. Expert Summary

Shorewood Hills is **not a builder-developed subdivision in the usual Integrity Homes sense** — it is a small, fully **incorporated municipality (Village of Shorewood Hills)**, Dane County, entirely surrounded by the City of Madison, sitting between the UW–Madison campus and Lake Mendota. `[PP+CL]`

- Formed from two earlier real-estate plats: **College Hills** (platted 1912–1915 per `[CL]`, or "around 1914" per `[PP]` — treat as the same plat, minor date-precision variance, not a conflict) and **Shorewood** (platted 1920s `[CL]`, or "post-WWI" `[PP]`). `[PP+CL]`
- **Incorporation date:** Village of Shorewood Hills incorporated **1927**, by Dane County Circuit Court order dated **July 31, 1927**. `[CL]` (Perplexity's leg describes the incorporation but does not give the 1927 date — not a conflict, just a gap Claude filled.)
- Because it's its own village government, it has **municipal zoning/building ordinances rather than a private-developer HOA**. `[PP+CL]`
- Housing stock is overwhelmingly single-family detached, mostly built **1914–1963** across the two historic districts (College Hills Historic District: contributing buildings 1914–1959; Shorewood Historic District: 1924–1963). `[PP+CL]`
- Prized for Lake Mendota adjacency, walkability, mature tree canopy, architectural character (Prairie School, Tudor, Arts & Crafts, International/Modern), and immediate proximity to the UW–Madison campus and UW Health's hospital campus. `[PP+CL]`
- **Distinctive addition from Claude leg, uncorroborated by Perplexity:** two Frank Lloyd Wright–associated structures sit in or near the village — the **John C. Pew House** (private residence) and the **First Unitarian Meeting House** (boundary placement not confirmed — flagged below). `[CL]`
- School district: **Madison Metropolitan School District (MMSD)**; Shorewood Hills Elementary is the local anchor school. `[PP+CL]`
- Market status, prices, days-on-market: **Pending MLS data from John** (SCWMLS required; do not substitute public-portal estimates). `[PP+CL]`

Because this is a century-old built-out village rather than a newly platted tract subdivision, "phase" and "builder" information will come from historic plats/district documentation and village ordinances, not a developer's phase map.

## C. Development and Phase History

### Verified history
- **College Hills plat:** created **1912–1915** `[CL]` / "around 1914" `[PP]` — same event, minor precision variance. Covered what is now part of Shorewood Hills; streets named after American/English colleges to appeal to university-affiliated buyers. `[PP+CL]`
- **Developer:** In 1913, **John C. McKenna** formed the **University Bay Land Co.**, purchased farmland with Lake Mendota/Picnic Point/UW campus views, and platted College Hills. Advertising emphasized "high class homes," enforced by deed restrictions. `[PP]`
- **Shorewood plat:** As demand grew after WWI, McKenna bought an additional strip of land and platted "Shorewood" as a companion subdivision along the lake and up "Blackhawk hill." `[PP+CL]`
- **Incorporation (1927):** The two plats were combined and incorporated as the Village of Shorewood Hills by Dane County Circuit Court order, July 31, 1927. `[CL]`
- **Build-out periods:** College Hills Historic District contributing buildings date 1914–1959; Shorewood Historic District platted 1922–1926 on Mendota Heights hill, homes built 1924–1963. "Although a number of homes were built between the wars, it was not until after WWII that the majority of houses were built." `[PP]`
- **Frank Lloyd Wright structures:** John C. Pew House (private residence) confirmed within the village; First Unitarian Meeting House commonly associated with the area but exact parcel boundary (inside village vs. just outside) **not confirmed — flag for verification**. `[CL]` — no corroboration or contradiction from Perplexity leg.
- **No later numbered phases:** Both legs agree this is a built-out, century-old village with essentially no vacant-lot new construction; any new homes are teardown/rebuilds on existing lots — a point of local friction (see Section K). `[PP+CL]`

### Future expansion
- Built-out village; no obvious remaining large tract for a new "phase." Infill redevelopment/teardown-rebuild is the only path for new product; not separately cataloged. `[PP+CL]`

**Not confident (both legs agree, gap remains open):** Names/dates of any builders involved in later infill (1970s–present); any distinct condo/townhome projects and whether specific named ones ("The Cove," "Marshall Court" per `[CL]`, single-source, uncorroborated) actually sit within village limits.

## G. HOA and Restrictions

- **No private HOA governs Shorewood Hills as a whole** — governance is via the **Village Code of Ordinances**, maintained at Village Hall, 810 Shorewood Blvd. `[PP+CL]` This is an important distinction to make explicit to buyers used to subdivision HOAs.
- Village governance structure: elected president and trustees, appointed clerk/treasurer/assessor (per a July 2024 governance-project draft). `[PP]`
- **Notable municipal building/zoning provisions (Claude leg, not found/covered by Perplexity leg):**
  - Site Survey required before lot grading, filling, or demolition (baseline for Floor Area/Height Limitation rules).
  - Erosion, Sediment and Runoff Ordinance — applies to almost any exterior addition/alteration.
  - **Dark Sky Ordinance** — restricts outdoor lighting fixtures to control light pollution.
  - Non-Conforming Structures may need Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA) variance before remodeling.
  - Zoning approval alone can take **90+ days** per village materials.
  - New "Zoning Review form" required in addition to standard building permit (village materials revised Aug 20, 2024). `[CL]`
- **Short-term rentals:** Perplexity's leg found a 2023 village meeting packet proposing STR limits — primary residence only, cap of 180 rental nights per 365 days; status (adopted vs. still proposed) needs re-check against current codified ordinances. `[PP]` Not found/covered independently by Claude's leg — not a conflict, just leg-specific coverage.
- **Framing point (Claude leg):** Do not describe this as simply "no HOA fee" without qualification — there's no HOA fee, but there is village property tax plus a stricter-than-typical municipal zoning/building review process that functions similarly to (in some ways more strictly than) an HOA architectural review committee. This nuance should not be flattened in marketing copy. `[CL]`

### Restrictions to verify (from Perplexity leg, consistent with the ordinance framework above)
Fence regulations, sheds/accessory structures, exterior-change rules in historic districts (Historic Preservation Commission review), rental/STR licensing detail, parking rules, pets/noise ordinances. `[PP]`
