## A. Expert Summary

Shorewood Hills is **not a subdivision in the usual sense** — it is a small, fully **incorporated municipality (Village of Shorewood Hills)**, entirely surrounded by the City of Madison, sitting between the UW–Madison campus and Lake Mendota. It was formed in 1927 from the merger of two earlier real-estate plats, College Hills (platted 1912–1915, adjacent to UW) and Shorewood (platted in the 1920s, along the lake and Blackhawk hill). Because it's its own village government, it has municipal zoning and building ordinances rather than a private-developer HOA — a distinction worth getting right for buyers. It's one of the most prestigious, architecturally significant, and expensive small residential communities in the Madison metro, prized for lake access, walkability (Walk Score ~87, Bike Score ~83 per one source), mature tree canopy, and immediate proximity to UW Health's University Hospital/American Family Children's Hospital campus, which is physically inside the village.

## C. Development and Phase History

- **College Hills** plat: laid out 1912–1915, immediately adjacent to the UW campus; earliest homes built in Prairie School, Arts & Crafts, and American Craftsman styles by architects of regional note. Its architectural unity is repeatedly cited as its defining visual character.
- **Shorewood** plat: laid out in the 1920s, running along Lake Mendota and up "Blackhawk hill."
- **1927**: The two plats were combined and incorporated as the Village of Shorewood Hills by Dane County Circuit Court order (July 31, 1927).
- Frank Lloyd Wright designed two notable structures within the village: the John C. Pew House (a private residence) and the First Unitarian Meeting House (not confirmed whether the Meeting House sits precisely inside village limits vs. just outside — **flag for verification**, since the First Unitarian Society building is commonly associated with Shorewood Hills/Madison's west side but exact parcel boundary wasn't independently confirmed in this pass).
- No later "phases" or new-construction sections were found — this is a built-out, century-old village with essentially no vacant-lot new construction; any new homes are teardown/rebuilds on existing lots, which is itself a point of local friction (see Section K).

## D. Location, Streets, and Boundaries

- **General location:** Madison's near-west side, wedged between the UW–Madison campus and Lake Mendota, entirely surrounded by the City of Madison.
- **Stated boundaries** (per aggregator description, treat as a general guide, not a legal plat description): bounded on the east by the UW–Madison campus, on the south by University Avenue, on the west by Madison's Indian Hills neighborhood, and on the north by Lake Mendota.
- **Streets confirmed as within or defining the village** (from village GIS/utility map PDFs and hydrant map): University Avenue, Lake Mendota Drive, Locust Drive, University Bay Drive, Edgehill Drive, Tallyho Lane, Topping Road, Oxford Road, Harvey Street, Columbia Road, Blackhawk Drive, Shorewood Boulevard.
- University Avenue is the main arterial, running from University Bay Drive west to Midvale Boulevard, connecting Shorewood Hills to downtown Madison to the east and to Middleton to the west.

## E. Schools

**District: Madison Metropolitan School District (MMSD).** MMSD serves the City of Madison, City of Fitchburg, Villages of Shorewood Hills and Maple Bluff, and the Towns of Blooming Grove and Burke.

- **Shorewood Hills Elementary School** — 1105 Shorewood Blvd, Madison, WI 53705. Serves PK–5, ~382 students per one profile. Repeatedly described as a top-performing MMSD elementary school. This is the school most associated with the village by name, but **school assignment is address-specific, not automatic** — MMSD draws attendance zones that do not necessarily match municipal boundaries exactly.
- Nearby MMSD elementary alternatives cited near the village: Van Hise Elementary (4747 Waukesha St) and Randall Elementary (1802 Regent St), each described as roughly 1 mile from Shorewood Hills Elementary — these serve adjacent Madison neighborhoods, not necessarily any part of Shorewood Hills itself.
- **Middle/high school assignments were not confirmed in this research pass** — flag as open item.
- **Mandatory caveat for any client-facing use:** Do not state or imply a guaranteed school assignment for any specific address. MMSD attendance zones should be verified per-address using MMSD's official "Find Your School" address-lookup tool before publishing anything address-specific.

## G. HOA and Restrictions

- **There is no private HOA governing Shorewood Hills as a whole** — because it is an incorporated village, the governing body is the Village of Shorewood Hills municipal government, and restrictions come from the **Village Code of Ordinances**, not a homeowners' association. This is an important distinction to make explicit to buyers used to subdivision HOAs.
- Notable municipal building/zoning provisions found:
  - **Site Survey** required before lot grading, filling, or demolition, to establish a baseline for Floor Area and Height Limitation rules.
  - **Erosion, Sediment and Runoff Ordinance** — applies to almost any exterior addition/alteration; stormwater control required.
  - **Dark Sky Ordinance** — restricts outdoor lighting fixtures (shielding/wattage) to control light pollution.
  - **Non-Conforming Structures**: a home that doesn't meet current zoning may need Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA) variance approval before remodeling.
  - **Building permit review timeline**: village materials state zoning approval alone can take 90+ days.
  - Village has been actively revising zoning review requirements for new single-family homes, additions, and any commercial/multi-family projects (a new "Zoning Review form" was cited as required in addition to the standard building permit, per village materials dated as recently revised August 20, 2024).
- **Do not describe this as "no HOA fee" without qualification** — there is no HOA fee, but there is a **village property tax** and a **stricter-than-typical municipal building/zoning review process** that functions similarly to (and in some ways more strictly than) an HOA architectural review committee. This nuance should not be flattened in marketing copy.

## H. Parks, Trails, and Amenities

- Village manages **nine parks**, totaling roughly 21 acres, scattered through the village.
- **Three of the nine parks, plus the Blackhawk Country Club, border Lake Mendota**, giving residents lake views/access.
- **McKenna Park** (commonly called "Shorewood Hills Beach") — one of the original parks from the 1922 Shorewood plat; has a bathing beach, pier, and the McKenna Park Boathouse (aka Shorewood Hills Boathouse), used to store canoes, dinghies, and sculls.
- **Frautschi Point** — an entrance point to UW–Madison's 300-acre **Lakeshore Nature Preserve**; this is a UW asset adjacent to/near the village, not a village-owned park. Do not describe it as a village amenity.
- **Blackhawk Country Club** borders the lake near the village — appears to be a private club, not confirmed as open to general village residents; **flag as not confident** on membership/access terms.
- No skate park, no dedicated village pool was found in this pass (village residents may use nearby Madison or UW facilities, not confirmed).

## L. Comparison With Nearby Subdivisions/Neighborhoods

*(Comparisons only — not a recommendation of one over another.)*

- **Nakoma**: Also a near-west-side historic Madison neighborhood (not a separate village — it's part of the City of Madison), ~4 miles to downtown, less than 3 miles to UW campus/hospital, ~800 historic homes, known for prairie-stone walls and decorative guardhouses; on the National Register of Historic Places. Distinct from Shorewood Hills — Nakoma is a City of Madison neighborhood; Shorewood Hills is its own incorporated village.
- **Dudgeon-Monroe**: Sits between Nakoma and the Vilas neighborhood; centered on walkable Monroe Street retail strip (coffee shops, boutiques, restaurants) and Lake Wingra/Wingra Park. A City of Madison neighborhood, not incorporated.
- **University Heights**: Adjacent west-side historic neighborhood, also near UW campus; a City of Madison neighborhood.
- **Sunset Village**: Another adjacent west-side Madison neighborhood, named alongside Shorewood Hills/University Heights/Dudgeon-Monroe/Vilas/Westlawn in one source as part of the same general west-side cluster.
- **Key structural difference to always state clearly**: Shorewood Hills is a legally separate incorporated village with its own government/zoning/tax base. Nakoma, Dudgeon-Monroe, University Heights, and Sunset Village are all neighborhoods *within* the City of Madison, not separate municipalities. This is a meaningfully different legal/governance status, not just a branding difference.

## M. Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Shorewood Hills part of the City of Madison?**
No — it is a separate, independently incorporated village, though it is entirely surrounded by the City of Madison and shares a school district (MMSD) and zip code (53705) region with nearby Madison neighborhoods.

**Does Shorewood Hills have an HOA?**
No private HOA. The village's own municipal government sets zoning/building rules through its Code of Ordinances, which in practice can be more involved than a typical HOA architectural review (see Section G).

**What school will my kids attend?**
Likely Shorewood Hills Elementary (MMSD) if inside its attendance zone, but this must be verified per-address via MMSD's official school-locator tool — do not assume based on village name alone.

**Is there lake access?**
Yes — three of the village's nine parks plus the Blackhawk Country Club border Lake Mendota; McKenna Park (Shorewood Hills Beach) has a public bathing beach, pier, and boathouse for village residents.

**How far is it from downtown Madison?**
Described in multiple sources as "minutes" from downtown/Capitol Square; a precise mile figure was not found from an authoritative source in this pass — pending confirmation (see Part 2 conveniences file for what could be estimated).

7. There's documented neighbor pushback (via Isthmus reporting) on at least one recent teardown/new-build proposal — good context for "know before you buy a teardown lot here" content.
8. UW Health's University Hospital and American Family Children's Hospital physically sit inside/adjacent to the village — many residents have hospital or university ties.
9. The village is genuinely tiny — under 700 housing units per the 2020 Census — so turnover and inventory are naturally low; good context for "why you don't see many listings here."
10. Three specific parks (not the whole shoreline) plus the country club touch Lake Mendota — not every street has "lake access" despite the village's lake-town reputation; parallels the Southbridge pool-access caution in this repo's other subdivisions.
