**IMPORTANT STRUCTURAL FLAG — READ FIRST [CL]:** "Maple Bluff" is not a subdivision or plat inside the City of Madison. It is the **independent, incorporated Village of Maple Bluff**, a separate municipality in Dane County bordering the City of Madison on three sides and Lake Mendota on the fourth. It has its own village government, police department, zoning code, and tax roll — never annexed to Madison (a 1929 annexation petition was not pursued; the village incorporated independently instead). Client-facing copy should say "Village of Maple Bluff" or "the Village of Maple Bluff, adjacent to Madison," not imply it's a Madison neighborhood/subdivision. This affects tax-bill framing, school-district framing (still MMSD, see Section E), and legal accuracy in marketing copy.

## A. Expert Summary [CL]

Maple Bluff is a small (0.69–0.70 sq mi), affluent, lakefront village on the eastern shore of Lake Mendota, immediately northeast of downtown Madison and directly north of the Sherman Avenue corridor. Incorporated in 1929 (originally as the "Village of Lakewood Bluff"), platted starting around 1915 by the Lakewood Land Company on land tied to Leonard Farwell's 1840s land holdings. One of the most exclusive residential addresses in the Madison metro — large lots, mature maple tree canopy, lake and bluff views, the private Maple Bluff Country Club (founded 1899), a public village beach park, and the site of the Wisconsin Governor's Mansion. Population ~1,368 (2020 census). Homes are architecturally distinct (1920s–1950s Colonial Revival, Cape Cod, Tudor Revival, plus midcentury ranches and some newer infill/rebuilds). **Not confident** on current total housing unit count — flag for MLS/Village verification.

## C. Development and Phase History [CL]

- Land originally associated with Leonard Farwell, who arrived in Madison in 1847 and acquired large tracts on the city's east side.
- Farwell Drive, the first permanent road through what is now Maple Bluff, completed 1897 by the Madison Park and Pleasure Drive Association.
- Maple Bluff Country Club founded 1899 — predates the residential village itself.
- By 1915, the Lakewood Land Company had platted most of present-day Maple Bluff. Madison city planner John Nolen originally proposed a denser plat (smaller lots, more streets) for the "Lakewood"/Lower Bluff section; the Lakewood Land Company rejected that plan in favor of the more spacious layout that exists today.
- Incorporated as the Village of Lakewood Bluff on May 17, 1929, after residents' earlier annexation petition to the City of Madison was not pursued to completion — driven at the time by inadequate rural infrastructure/services. **Not confident** on the exact renaming date to "Maple Bluff" from "Lakewood Bluff" — flag for verification.
- Housing stock reflects mostly early-to-mid 20th century construction (1920s–1950s) with later infill: midcentury modern ranches and some early-2000s and newer builds/rebuilds mentioned by real estate sources. This is **not** phase-by-phase subdivision data — there is no "Phase 1/Phase 2" plat structure like a modern platted subdivision; Maple Bluff developed organically over a century, not in discrete builder phases.

## G. HOA and Restrictions [CL]

There is **no traditional homeowners' association** in the conventional subdivision-HOA sense — Maple Bluff is governed as its own municipality with a Village Board, its own police department, and its own zoning code (Chapter 225 of the Village's Municipal Code), rather than a private HOA with CC&Rs.

- Zoning ordinance: Chapter 225, comprehensively updated and adopted by the Village Board on **June 12, 2025**. Divides the village into zoning districts with district-specific rules on land use, setbacks, building design, and signage.
- Floodplain zoning regulations/maps updated per WDNR/FEMA requirements (specific adoption date not confirmed — flag).
- Short-term rental restriction (confirmed): a single-family residence **may not be rented for 6 or fewer days**. Rentals of more than 6 but fewer than 30 consecutive days to a single family are classified as a "medium-term rental residence" and require a Village license.
- Village residents get preferential access to Maple Bluff Country Club membership (half the standard initiation fee; new residents can bypass the golf waiting list if they apply within 30 days of establishing residency) — a club-membership perk, not a legal HOA obligation; MBCC membership itself is optional/private.

**Flag:** Any specific setback, height, or accessory-structure rule referenced for a client should be pulled from the live Chapter 225 zoning ordinance text (villageofmaplebluff.com / eCode360), not summarized secondhand, since this is legally consequential content.

11. The Robert M. La Follette House (a nationally significant political-history site) is located in Maple Bluff, not downtown Madison as some might assume.
12. The village underwent a full zoning ordinance rewrite adopted June 12, 2025 — current buyers/builders are operating under a genuinely new code, not the older one referenced in older articles.
