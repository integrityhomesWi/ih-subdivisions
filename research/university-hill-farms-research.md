## A. Expert Summary [CL]

University Hill Farms — locally shortened to "Hill Farms" — is an established, built-out mid-century neighborhood on Madison's near west side, not a new-construction subdivision. It was developed starting in the 1950s when the University of Wisconsin subdivided a former experimental farm into a planned, self-contained suburb, in partnership with the City of Madison. Part of the neighborhood — the University Hill Farms Historic District — was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 2015 for its mid-century Modern Movement architecture (Ranch and Contemporary styles, including one Frank Lloyd Wright–designed house) and for its significance as a planned community. This is fundamentally different from the newer-construction Dane County subdivisions in this project's other cut sheets: there are no active phases, no builder, and no lots for sale — it's resale housing in a 70-year-old neighborhood. No meaningful name-collision risk was found — every search result returned was specific to Madison, WI. Do not confuse it with the separate "Hill Farms State Office Building" redevelopment site (a large mixed-use state/private project on the neighborhood's north edge) — that's a government office/mixed-use project embedded near the neighborhood's boundary, not the residential neighborhood itself, though directly relevant to nearby-amenities questions (Sections H/I).

## C. Development and Phase History [CL]

- Land origin: part of a University of Wisconsin experimental/agricultural research farm.
- **1953**: Wisconsin state legislature authorized sale of the UW farmland for development (single-source, homes.com/Public School Review aggregator — not independently corroborated).
- **Starting in the 1950s**: UW and the City of Madison collaborated to subdivide the farm into a planned, self-contained suburban community — intended to include housing, schools, parks, churches, shopping, and offices in one walkable neighborhood, per the aggregator summary.
- Homes built are predominantly mid-century: Ranch, split-level, Contemporary, bi-level, and some Colonial Revival styles, largely dating to the 1950s–1960s build-out era. **Not confident** on the exact construction date range for the neighborhood as a whole — the National Register nomination would have this, but the primary NR document (wisconsinhistory.org NR2471) was not directly read in this research pass.
- **2015**: A defined "University Hill Farms Historic District" (a subset of the broader neighborhood, not necessarily every parcel within the association's boundaries) was added to the National Register of Historic Places.
- **Gap:** fully built-out, decades-old neighborhood — no active "phase" construction the way a new subdivision would have. No source found gives an exact build-out completion year.
- **Not confident**: whether the Historic District boundary and the broader neighborhood/association boundary are identical — historic districts are often a subset, and this was not confirmed. Treat "Historic District" and "neighborhood" as related but not necessarily coextensive until verified.

## G. HOA and Restrictions [CL]

- The **University Hill Farms Neighborhood Association** is explicitly described as "an optional cooperative among neighborhood residents" — **not a mandatory HOA** governing architectural review, assessments, or deed-recorded restrictions the way a typical subdivision HOA would. Membership requires only an annual fee (reported inconsistently as $10 vs. $20 — see Section B flag) and residing within the defined boundary.
- **No source found describing deed restrictions, architectural control covenants, or mandatory assessments** for the neighborhood as a whole. Given the age of the neighborhood (1950s platting) there may be legacy deed restrictions on individual parcels, but this pass found no documentation of them.
- **Separately, the Hill Farm Swim Club is its own private membership organization** (stock-share model: reported $700 stock purchase + annual dues + a separate swim/dive team fee) — optional, pay-to-join amenity access, not a neighborhood-wide HOA fee. **Do not apply the swim club's fee structure to the neighborhood association, or vice versa** — two separate organizations, two separate fee structures, easy to conflate.
- **Not confident** on current-year dollar figures for either the association dues or swim club dues/stock price — verify directly (hillfarms.org/membership and hillfarmswimclub.org) before quoting numbers to a client.
