## Caption Structure

Every caption follows this framework:

### 1. Hook (first 1–2 lines)
The first line is everything — it's what shows before "…more." It must create enough curiosity or value to earn the tap.

Effective hooks:
- A bold, specific claim: "Homes in Waunakee are selling 12 days faster than last quarter."
- A counterintuitive take: "The worst time to sell your home might not be when you think."
- A direct question: "What's the #1 thing buyers notice in the first 10 seconds of a showing?"
- A pattern interrupt: "Stop staging your dining room. Here's why."
- A personal opener: "I showed 4 homes yesterday. One thing every buyer said…"
- A data lead: "Dane County inventory is down 18% from last year. Here's what that means for you."

Avoid generic openers: "Thinking about selling?" "Just listed!" "Happy Monday!" These get scrolled past.

### 2. Body (3–8 lines)
Deliver on the hook's promise. Rules:
- One idea per caption. If you have three ideas, that's three posts.
- Short paragraphs — 1–2 sentences max per paragraph. Use line breaks for scannability.
- Be specific. Name the neighborhood, the feature, the number. Specificity beats generality.
- Write conversationally. Read it out loud — if it sounds written, rewrite it.
- Include real value: a tip, an insight, a perspective, data. The reader should learn something or feel something.
- No filler. Every line should earn its place.
- Match John's laid-back, knowledgeable tone — informative without being salesy.

### 3. CTA (last 1–2 lines)
Close with one clear action. Match the CTA to the content type:
- Listing post: "DM me for details" or "Link in bio for the full listing"
- Educational post: "Save this for later" or "Share this with someone who needs to hear it"
- Engagement post: "Drop your answer below" or "Tell me in the comments"
- Lead generation post: "Comment [KEYWORD] and I'll send you [resource]"
- Hero content: "If you're a veteran, first responder, teacher, or healthcare worker — DM me about Reward Our Heroes"

Only include a keyword CTA when there's a clear lead magnet or gated resource to deliver. Don't force it.

## Content Types and How To Handle Each

**Listing post:** Lead with what makes this property different — not beds/baths (that's in the MLS). Lead with the lifestyle, the neighborhood, or the one feature a buyer would remember. Name the specific Dane County location.

**Market update:** Lead with the single most surprising or relevant stat. Frame it for whichever audience John specifies (buyers hear data differently than sellers). Use only data John provides — never fabricate statistics.

**Educational/tip:** Lead with the problem, then give the solution. Keep it actionable — the reader should be able to do something with this today.

**Personal/behind-the-scenes:** Lead with the moment, not the lesson. Tell the story first, then extract the takeaway at the end (or don't — sometimes the story is enough). John's military background and firefighter experience can inform these when relevant.

**Community/local:** Name the place, the event, the business. Be specific. "Best coffee in Sun Prairie" beats "supporting local businesses." Reference specific Dane County neighborhoods and landmarks.

**Reward Our Heroes:** Lead with the mission — serving those who serve us. Veterans, first responders, teachers, healthcare workers. Focus on community impact, not discounts. Check `accounts.md` for the confirmed ROH Instagram handle before tagging.

## Formatting Rules

- No emojis in the opening hook. Emojis are fine in the body or CTA — use them sparingly and intentionally, not as decoration.
- No hashtags in the caption body. If hashtags are wanted, add them as the very last element, separated by a line break. Limit to 5–10 relevant hashtags.
- Do not generate trending or "best" hashtags. Claude does not have access to real-time hashtag performance data. Use straightforward, relevant hashtags based on the content topic and location.
- Use line breaks between paragraphs for readability.
- Keep total caption length between 100–300 words for most posts. Carousels can go longer if the content supports it.
