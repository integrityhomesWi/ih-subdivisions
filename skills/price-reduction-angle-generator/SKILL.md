# Price Reduction Angle Generator

Help John have the hardest conversation in the business — "we need to reduce the price" — with data behind every word. This skill does not write a script. It produces a positioning statement and supporting data points John can use in person, on the phone, in a text, or in an email.

## Intake

Collect the following. Ask in one message:

1. **Subject property:** Address or reference, current list price, total days on market
2. **Property details:** Beds, baths, square footage, year built, lot size, key features or upgrades
3. **Feedback received:** Any showing feedback, buyer agent comments, or objections heard so far
4. **Seller context:** Any known seller constraints or sensitivities (timeline, emotional attachment, previous price adjustments, relocation deadline)
5. **Comparable data:** Ask for a spreadsheet of recently sold and active comparable listings (ideally last 30–90 days, same area, similar property type)

If comparable data is not provided, ask for it. Do not proceed without data — this skill is only useful when grounded in real numbers.

## Dane County Market Context

When analyzing price reduction positioning, consider these local factors:

### What's moving in Dane County
- Move-in ready homes priced at or below median for their neighborhood
- Properties with updated kitchens and bathrooms
- Homes with three-car garages in suburban markets (Waunakee, DeForest, Sun Prairie)
- Walkout basements and finished lower levels

### What's sitting
- Overpriced per square foot relative to recent closed comps
- Dated finishes in mid-to-upper price brackets
- Single-car garage in areas where two-car is standard
- Busy road locations without a price discount to compensate

### Price brackets to watch
- Entry-level ($300K–$450K): Typically competitive, multiple offers possible
- Mid-range ($450K–$650K): More DOM-sensitive, pricing precision matters
- Move-up/luxury ($650K+): Slower absorption, buyers more selective

## Analysis Process

### Step 1: What's Moving
From the uploaded comps, identify the properties that sold. Note:
- The price range where closings are concentrated
- Average DOM for sold properties vs. the subject's DOM
- List-to-sale ratios for sold properties
- Features or price brackets that sold fastest

### Step 2: What's Sitting
Identify active listings that have been on market for 30+ days. Note:
- What price point are they clustered at
- How they compare to the subject property in features and price
- Any pattern in what's not selling (overpriced per square foot, lacking key features, wrong price bracket for the condition)

### Step 3: Where the Subject Fits
Place the subject property in context:
- Is it priced above, at, or below the range where properties are actually closing?
- How does its price per square foot compare to sold comps?
- Are there feature gaps that explain the lack of activity (e.g., no garage in an area where every sold comp had one)?
- What would the data suggest as a competitive price position?

## Output

Produce three things:

### 1. The Angle (2–3 sentences)
A clear, seller-facing positioning statement that frames the adjustment as market-aligned strategy. This is the talking point John walks in with. It should:
- Reference a specific data point (not a feeling)
- Frame the adjustment as a way to compete, not a concession
- Avoid blame, pressure, or urgency language
- Match John's laid-back, data-driven communication style

**Example:** "The homes that sold in Westport this month all closed between $475,000 and $510,000. At $549,000, your home is priced above every comparable that actually went under contract. Adjusting to $515,000 positions you at the top of the range buyers are actively choosing from — competitive, not desperate."

### 2. Supporting Data Points (3–4 bullets)
Specific numbers from the comp analysis that back up the angle. These are the receipts:
- "Sold comps in the last 60 days: median sale price $492,000, median DOM 18 days"
- "Your listing: 47 days on market at $549,000 with 2 showings in the last 2 weeks"
- "Active competitors at similar price points: [count] listings, average DOM [X] days — also not moving"
- "Price per square foot for sold comps: $168–$182. Your current price per sqft: $196."

### 3. Suggested Next Step (1 sentence)
A recommended action: adjust to a specific price range, schedule a strategy call, or run a new comp analysis after two weeks at the adjusted price.

## Rules

- The angle must be rooted in the uploaded data. No fabricated statistics or assumed market conditions.
- Never tell John what the "right" price is — frame all price suggestions as "where the market is responding" or "where buyers are engaging."
- Tone must be empathetic but honest. John's credibility depends on telling the truth without being harsh.
- Do not use urgency or fear tactics ("if you don't reduce now, you'll never sell").
- Do not reference the seller's personal situation in the positioning statement — that's for John to manage. The data speaks for itself.
- Comply with Fair Housing: no analysis by demographics or protected class.
- Match the laid-back, knowledgeable tone of Integrity Homes — informative, not alarmist.
