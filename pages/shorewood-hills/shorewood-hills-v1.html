About the Village

A Village Inside the City.
Not a Neighborhood — a Municipality.

Shorewood Hills isn't a subdivision in the usual sense — it's its own small, independently incorporated municipality, entirely surrounded by the City of Madison, sitting between the UW–Madison campus and Lake Mendota. In 1913, John C. McKenna formed the University Bay Land Co. and platted College Hills on farmland with Lake Mendota, Picnic Point, and UW campus views. As demand grew after WWI, McKenna platted a companion subdivision, Shorewood, along the lake and up what locals called "Blackhawk hill." The two plats were combined and incorporated as the Village of Shorewood Hills by Dane County Circuit Court order on July 31, 1927.

Because it's its own village government, Shorewood Hills has municipal zoning and building ordinances rather than a private-developer HOA — a distinction worth understanding before you buy here. Most homes were built between 1914 and 1963 across the village's two historic districts, College Hills and Shorewood, in styles ranging from Colonial Revival and Tudor to Prairie School and Arts & Crafts. It's a built-out, century-old village: there's essentially no vacant land left, so any new home here is a lot-by-lot teardown-and-rebuild project rather than tract new construction.

"Not a neighborhood — a legally separate municipality with its own government, inside the larger Madison metro."

The village manages roughly nine parks totaling about 21 acres, with three of them — plus Blackhawk Country Club — bordering Lake Mendota. McKenna Park, also known as Shorewood Hills Beach, dates to the original 1922 Shorewood plat and has a public bathing beach, pier, and boathouse. Frank Lloyd Wright's John C. Pew House, a private residence, sits inside the village — a genuine architectural landmark most Madison-area buyers don't expect to find here.

Common Questions

Shorewood Hills FAQ

❓ FAQ ▾
Is Shorewood Hills part of the City of Madison?
No. Shorewood Hills is a separate, independently incorporated village — incorporated in 1927 — even though it's entirely surrounded by the City of Madison and shares the 53705 zip code and the Madison Metropolitan School District.
What schools serve Shorewood Hills?
The Madison Metropolitan School District. Shorewood Hills Elementary (1105 Shorewood Blvd, PK–5, roughly 380–405 students) is the confirmed assigned elementary school. Middle and high school assignment is reported elsewhere as Hamilton Middle School (with a Wright Middle lottery option) and Madison West High School, but that specific assignment was only independently sourced once in our research and should be verified directly against MMSD's official attendance-area map before you rely on it for a specific address.
Does Shorewood Hills have an HOA?
No private HOA governs the village. Instead, Shorewood Hills is governed by its own Village Code of Ordinances, maintained at Village Hall, 810 Shorewood Boulevard. In practice this can be more involved than a typical HOA architectural review — zoning approval alone can take 90+ days, and there's a Site Survey requirement, an Erosion, Sediment and Runoff Ordinance, and a Dark Sky Ordinance restricting outdoor lighting.
Is there a neighborhood pool, beach, or lake access?
Yes. McKenna Park, also known as Shorewood Hills Beach, has a public bathing beach, a pier, and the McKenna Park Boathouse for canoes, dinghies, and sculls — it dates to the original 1922 Shorewood plat. Three of the village's nine parks, plus Blackhawk Country Club, border Lake Mendota, though not every street in the village has direct lake frontage.
Can I operate a short-term rental in Shorewood Hills?
A 2023 village meeting packet proposed limiting short-term rentals to a primary residence, capped at 180 rental nights per 365 days. Whether that proposal has since been formally adopted was not confirmed in our research — check the current codified ordinances at the Village or shorewoodhillswi.gov before assuming a rule applies.
What are homes selling for in Shorewood Hills?
Sold-price, median-price, and days-on-market figures for Shorewood Hills are pending confirmation against SCWMLS data and are not yet published on this page. Contact John Reuter at 608-669-4226 for current, MLS-sourced figures.
How old are the homes in Shorewood Hills, and what should I expect to maintain?
Most homes were built between 1914 and 1963 across the village's two historic districts. Expect age-related maintenance considerations — foundations, plumbing, wiring, and insulation typical of housing stock from that era — plus historic-district design review on many exterior changes.
Is there room for new construction in Shorewood Hills?
Very little. Shorewood Hills is a built-out, century-old village with no significant remaining vacant tracts. New homes are almost always lot-by-lot teardown-and-rebuild projects rather than tract new construction, and at least one recent proposal drew organized neighbor opposition, so buyers planning a teardown should expect a longer, more scrutinized process.
How walkable is Shorewood Hills to the elementary school and UW campus?
Highly walkable in practice — Shorewood Hills Elementary sits inside the village, and the UW–Madison campus and UW Health's hospital campus are immediately adjacent to the village boundary. A specific Walk Score figure exists in one source but was not independently corroborated, so we're not citing a number here.

Common Questions

Shorewood Hills FAQ

❓ FAQ ▾
Is Shorewood Hills part of the City of Madison?
No. Shorewood Hills is a separate, independently incorporated village — incorporated in 1927 — even though it's entirely surrounded by the City of Madison and shares the 53705 zip code and the Madison Metropolitan School District.
What schools serve Shorewood Hills?
The Madison Metropolitan School District. Shorewood Hills Elementary (1105 Shorewood Blvd, PK–5, roughly 380–405 students) is the confirmed assigned elementary school. Middle and high school assignment is reported elsewhere as Hamilton Middle School (with a Wright Middle lottery option) and Madison West High School, but that specific assignment was only independently sourced once in our research and should be verified directly against MMSD's official attendance-area map before you rely on it for a specific address.
Does Shorewood Hills have an HOA?
No private HOA governs the village. Instead, Shorewood Hills is governed by its own Village Code of Ordinances, maintained at Village Hall, 810 Shorewood Boulevard. In practice this can be more involved than a typical HOA architectural review — zoning approval alone can take 90+ days, and there's a Site Survey requirement, an Erosion, Sediment and Runoff Ordinance, and a Dark Sky Ordinance restricting outdoor lighting.
Is there a neighborhood pool, beach, or lake access?
Yes. McKenna Park, also known as Shorewood Hills Beach, has a public bathing beach, a pier, and the McKenna Park Boathouse for canoes, dinghies, and sculls — it dates to the original 1922 Shorewood plat. Three of the village's nine parks, plus Blackhawk Country Club, border Lake Mendota, though not every street in the village has direct lake frontage.
Can I operate a short-term rental in Shorewood Hills?
A 2023 village meeting packet proposed limiting short-term rentals to a primary residence, capped at 180 rental nights per 365 days. Whether that proposal has since been formally adopted was not confirmed in our research — check the current codified ordinances at the Village or shorewoodhillswi.gov before assuming a rule applies.
What are homes selling for in Shorewood Hills?
Sold-price, median-price, and days-on-market figures for Shorewood Hills are pending confirmation against SCWMLS data and are not yet published on this page. Contact John Reuter at 608-669-4226 for current, MLS-sourced figures.
How old are the homes in Shorewood Hills, and what should I expect to maintain?
Most homes were built between 1914 and 1963 across the village's two historic districts. Expect age-related maintenance considerations — foundations, plumbing, wiring, and insulation typical of housing stock from that era — plus historic-district design review on many exterior changes.
Is there room for new construction in Shorewood Hills?
Very little. Shorewood Hills is a built-out, century-old village with no significant remaining vacant tracts. New homes are almost always lot-by-lot teardown-and-rebuild projects rather than tract new construction, and at least one recent proposal drew organized neighbor opposition, so buyers planning a teardown should expect a longer, more scrutinized process.
How walkable is Shorewood Hills to the elementary school and UW campus?
Highly walkable in practice — Shorewood Hills Elementary sits inside the village, and the UW–Madison campus and UW Health's hospital campus are immediately adjacent to the village boundary. A specific Walk Score figure exists in one source but was not independently corroborated, so we're not citing a number here.
