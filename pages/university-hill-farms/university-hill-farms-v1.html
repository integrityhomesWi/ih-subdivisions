About the Neighborhood

From University Farmland
to Mid-Century Landmark

University Hill Farms sits on land that was once a University of Wisconsin experimental farm. The Wisconsin state legislature reportedly authorized its sale for development in 1953, and the University and the City of Madison subdivided the land starting in the 1950s into a planned, self-contained community — housing, schools, parks, and shopping meant to sit within walking distance of each other, decades before "walkable neighborhood" became a marketing phrase.

Part of the neighborhood — the University Hill Farms Historic District — was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 2015, recognized both for its mid-century Modern Movement architecture (Ranch and Contemporary styles, including at least one reported Frank Lloyd Wright–designed house) and for its significance as a planned community. This is a fundamentally different kind of neighborhood from the newer-construction Dane County subdivisions elsewhere on this site: there's no active builder, no lots for sale, and no phase construction — just a 70-year-old resale market.

"A planned 1950s community built to put homes, schools, parks, and shopping within walking distance of each other — the original intent, not a later add-on."

The neighborhood sits on Madison's near west side, bounded by Regent Street and Sheboygan Avenue to the north, Mineral Point Road to the south, Segoe Road to the east, and Rosa Road and North Whitney Way to the west — just north of Hilldale Shopping Center's block. A large mixed-use redevelopment, Madison Yards at Hill Farms, is actively under construction on the neighborhood's north edge at Segoe Road and Sheboygan Avenue; buyers should know this is an ongoing, multi-year project, not a settled boundary.

University Hill Farms FAQ

Is University Hill Farms a new subdivision?
No. It's an established Madison neighborhood built out mostly in the 1950s and 1960s on former University of Wisconsin farmland. There's no active builder or new-construction phase — every home here is a resale.
Is there an HOA in University Hill Farms?
There's a voluntary University Hill Farms Neighborhood Association — not a mandatory recorded HOA — with a modest annual membership fee. Reported figures for the current fee conflict between sources ($10 vs. $20 per household), so confirm the exact amount directly at hillfarms.org before joining.
What schools does University Hill Farms feed into?
Homes here are zoned to the Madison Metropolitan School District, reportedly Van Hise or Glenn Stephens Elementary, Hamilton Middle School, and Madison West High School. Elementary assignment is address-specific — Stephens Elementary has reported "island zone" boundaries, so always verify a specific address through MMSD's official attendance-area tool before assuming a school assignment.
Is there a neighborhood pool in University Hill Farms?
There's a private membership swim club, Hill Farm Swim Club, not a free or included amenity. It's a separate organization from the neighborhood association, with its own stock-share membership structure — don't confuse the two groups' dues.
What's the big construction project near University Hill Farms?
Madison Yards at Hill Farms — a large mixed-use redevelopment of the former Hill Farms State Office Building site at Segoe Road and Sheboygan Avenue. A new state office tower and a Whole Foods Market are open; the full originally proposed retail, hotel, and housing scope should not be treated as fully built without current verification.

What is University Hill Farms in Madison, Wisconsin?

University Hill Farms — often called "Hill Farms" — is an established residential neighborhood on Madison, Wisconsin's near west side, in Dane County. Built starting in the 1950s on former University of Wisconsin farmland, it's known for mid-century Ranch and Contemporary architecture and its 2015 National Register-listed historic district. It's resale-only, with no active builder and no published price range — MLS-sourced sold-price data is pending. Homes feed into Madison's MMSD district (Van Hise or Stephens Elementary, Hamilton Middle, Madison West High), about a mile from Hilldale's Metcalfe's Market.
