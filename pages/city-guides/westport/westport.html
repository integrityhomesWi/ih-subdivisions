01 — Origin

An Irish Town That Gave Birth to Waunakee

Westport is older than most of what surrounds it. The town that people now drive through on the way to somewhere else is the parent, not the child — Waunakee was literally carved out of it.

Westport was settled beginning in the 1840s largely by Irish immigrants, and they named it after Westport in County Mayo, Ireland — the home many of them had left behind. That heritage is baked into the town's identity in a way that most Dane County suburbs, with their developer-chosen subdivision names, simply don't have.

In 1893, the Village of Waunakee was carved out of the Town of Westport. Over the following century, the cities of Madison and Middleton annexed other portions, and a stretch of the former Town of Madison on the north shore of Lake Mendota was transferred into Westport. What's left today is an unusual shape: an "island" town, surrounded and bordered by Madison, Middleton, Waunakee, and DeForest.

"Westport is the parent municipality of Waunakee — not a suburb of it. That history is why the town has fought so hard to keep its own borders."

That island geography is the single most important fact about Westport, and it explains almost everything else — why the lots are larger, why there's no commercial downtown, why so much of the shoreline is protected, and why, in 2024, the town's leadership decided it was time to stop being annexed one parcel at a time and become a village of its own.

02 — Why Westport

The Case for the North Shore

Westport isn't for everyone, and it isn't trying to be. Here's who it's for — and the specific reasons buyers choose it over the denser, newer subdivisions just up the road.

What is it like living in Westport, WI? Westport offers larger lots, waterfront and rural settings on the north shore of Lake Mendota, and access to top-rated Waunakee schools — with Governor Nelson State Park in the backyard and downtown Madison about ten minutes away. It trades commercial convenience and density for space, quiet, and lake access, making it one of the most country-feeling addresses inside easy reach of the city.

01

Larger Lots, Country Feel

Where Waunakee and Middleton are building family-focused subdivisions on standard lots, Westport is where you find acreage, bigger yards, and a genuine rural setting — without leaving the metro. It's the "more land, more quiet" answer inside a 15-minute commute.

02

Lake Mendota on the North Shore

Waterfront and near-water living on the quieter, less-developed side of Wisconsin's second-largest lake. Marinas, boat access, and Governor Nelson State Park's sandy beach — with the Capitol dome visible across the water — anchor the town's identity.

03

Top-Rated Schools, Two Districts

Most of Westport feeds the Waunakee Community School District — #7 in Wisconsin overall — with a portion in Middleton-Cross Plains, another top-rated system. You get elite schools without living inside either village's density. Verify the line by parcel.

04

Ten Minutes to Everything

Downtown Madison, the Capitol, and UW are roughly ten miles south. Dane County Regional Airport is about five miles. You're rural at home and urban-adjacent the moment you leave the driveway — a rare combination this close to the lake.

03 — Where You'll Live

The Settings & Subdivisions of Westport

Westport is best understood as a set of settings — waterfront, rural-residential, master-planned edge, and senior living — with a number of named subdivisions within them. Here's how the town breaks down for buyers, followed by the developments people search for by name.

Lake Mendota Waterfront & Near-Shore. The north-shore corridor along the lake is Westport's premium setting — waterfront homes, marinas, and near-water lots with some of the quietest lake access in the county. Inventory here is limited and turns over slowly; when a true waterfront parcel comes available, it draws serious attention fast.

Rural-Residential & Acreage. The heart of Westport: larger lots, country roads, and homes on real land. This is the profile buyers come to Westport for and increasingly can't find in Waunakee or Middleton. Expect a range from modest older farmhouses to custom homes on multiple acres.

Master-Planned Edge. Portions of larger master-planned development straddle the Middleton and Westport line north of Lake Mendota, offering a more amenity-driven lifestyle within the town's lower-density surroundings. School district and jurisdiction vary lot by lot along these edges — confirm before writing.

Senior & Downsizing Living. Westport has a notably higher share of retirees than Waunakee, supported in part by senior-living options such as Westshire Village. For downsizing buyers who want lake-country quiet with services nearby in Waunakee, it's a genuine draw.

04 — Schools

Two Top-Rated Districts, One Town

Westport's biggest school advantage is also its biggest gotcha: it straddles two of Dane County's best districts, and the line does not follow mailing addresses. Verify by parcel, every time.

Waunakee Community School District serves most of Westport — 4K through 12th grade, seven schools, a 13:1 student-teacher ratio, and a 95.9% graduation rate. It's ranked #7 in Wisconsin overall and #1 in the state for athletics. For the full picture of the district, schools, and boundaries, see the Living in Waunakee guide.

Middleton-Cross Plains Area School District serves a portion of Westport, primarily on the southern and western edges. It is one of Wisconsin's most consistently top-ranked public systems, feeding Middleton High School in the Big Eight Conference.

The Town's own incorporation FAQ confirms it plainly: Westport students attend either the Waunakee Community or Middleton-Cross Plains district depending on where they live — and that would not change if the town incorporates. There's also a private option nearby: St. John the Baptist Catholic School in Waunakee (K–7), whose parish congregation covers Dane, Lodi, Waunakee, and Westport.

Buyer note: Two top districts serving one town means the school assignment — and the mill rate that comes with it — can change from one parcel to the next. Never assume a Westport address is Waunakee schools. Confirm the assigned schools and district at the parcel level, through the applicable district's official address-lookup tool, before writing an offer.

05 — The Village Question

Westport's Bid to Become a Village

The most important thing happening in Westport right now isn't a new subdivision — it's a fight over the town's future as a place. Here's the honest, current status.

Westport is an island town, surrounded and bordered by Madison, Middleton, Waunakee, and DeForest — all of which can control land use near their borders and have annexed pieces of Westport over the years. To stop that, the town is trying to incorporate as a village, which would fix its borders permanently and end annexation.

The timeline so far: the Town Board voted unanimously on September 23, 2024 to support the petition, and the petition was filed with the Dane County Circuit Court on October 4, 2024. A circuit court judge found the petition met the statutory criteria in December 2024. The town then filed with the state Incorporation Review Board in 2025, and in early 2026 a cooperative boundary agreement was approved by all the surrounding communities — an agreement that becomes null and void if incorporation doesn't happen.

The petition is now before the Wisconsin Department of Administration's Incorporation Review Board. If it clears that review, Westport residents would decide the question in a referendum, which the town hoped could land in late 2026 or the spring 2027 elections.

"The whole point of incorporation is to stop being annexed one parcel at a time — and to protect the Lake Mendota watershed while they're at it."

What it means for buyers: incorporation is not final until that referendum passes. It could change borders, governance, and eventually services. If you're buying near the town's edges, ask your agent for the current status and how the proposed boundary affects the specific parcel — this is a live, moving situation.

06 — Parks & Outdoors

Governor Nelson and the North Shore

Westport's outdoor infrastructure punches far above a town of its size — because one of the best state parks in the region sits right on its shoreline.

Governor Nelson State Park. The crown jewel — on the Lake Mendota shoreline inside Westport. A sandy swimming beach with a separate pet swim area, two boat launches, eight-plus miles of trails through prairie and woodland, and Native American effigy mounds. On a clear day, the Capitol dome is visible across the water to the south. Groomed cross-country ski trails in winter make it a year-round anchor of the Westport lifestyle.

Mendota & Yahara Heights County Parks. Mendota County Park sits on the lake shore within Westport with boat launches, a beach area, and a campground. Yahara Heights County Park, along the Yahara River, adds a fully fenced off-leash dog park, hiking trails, and canoe/kayak launch access. Together they give the town deep public water and trail access.

Westport Prairie & conservancy land. Westport Prairie, a 227-acre wildlife area managed by Groundswell Conservancy with about 1.5 miles of trails, sits east of Waunakee off Bong Road. Combined with the Cherokee Marsh wetland complex just off the town's edge, Westport is wrapped in protected open space on multiple sides.

For village-scale parks, trails, splash pads, and the Wednesday farmers market, Westport residents lean on neighboring Waunakee — 27 parks and a connected trail network, covered in detail in the Living in Waunakee guide.

08 — Veterans & Military Families

A Strong Fit for Those Who Served

Westport's position in the north-Madison corridor puts it inside one of the most veteran-supportive regions in Wisconsin — with the VA hospital, Truax Field, and one of the country's strongest veteran tax benefits all within reach.

The William S. Middleton Memorial Veterans Hospital anchors veteran healthcare for the region just south in Madison, and Truax Field's 115th Fighter Wing — transitioning to the F-35A — is a short drive southeast. Many Guard members and retirees choose the north-Madison towns specifically for the space and the schools.

Wisconsin 100% Disability Veteran Property Tax Credit: veterans rated 100% permanent and total disabled by the VA — and eligible unremarried surviving spouses — may qualify for a state income tax credit equal to property taxes paid on the principal residence. 09 — Utilities & Infrastructure

Well, Septic, and the Rural-Living Checklist

This is where Westport differs most from a village subdivision — and where buyers relocating from a city get surprised. Rural and lakeside living comes with infrastructure questions you have to ask up front.

Water & septic. Many Westport properties — especially rural and near-shore parcels — are on private well and septic rather than municipal water and sewer. That means well-water testing, septic inspection, and understanding maintenance and replacement costs are essential parts of due diligence. Some edges of town near Waunakee or Middleton may be on municipal service; verify by parcel. Where the town does provide utility service, water is billed monthly and sewer semiannually, in March and September.

Shoreland zoning. Parcels near Lake Mendota fall under Dane County shoreland-zoning rules — which the county administers regardless of jurisdiction — governing setbacks, impervious surface, tree removal, and what you can build or add near the water. If lake access is the reason you're buying, understand these rules before you write; they can meaningfully affect what a waterfront lot can become.

Internet. Coverage in a rural town is more variable than in a built-out subdivision. TDS and Spectrum serve much of the north-Madison corridor, but fiber and speed availability can change parcel to parcel. If you work from home, confirm actual available speeds at the specific address before closing.

Refuse & radon. Refuse and recycling are handled through GFL — refuse weekly, recycling every other week, carts out by 6:00 a.m. And Dane County is EPA Radon Zone 1, the highest-priority zone: test every home regardless of age or basement type; mitigation is straightforward and inexpensive when caught at inspection.

10 — Cost of Living

What Westport Actually Costs

Westport tracks with the premium north-Madison market — but the numbers depend heavily on setting (waterfront vs. rural-residential) and on which school district a parcel falls in.

Is Westport, WI expensive? Westport is a premium Dane County market. Waterfront and acreage properties command the top of the range, while more modest rural-residential homes sit lower. Property taxes run roughly 1.6–1.8% of assessed value depending on the school district, and well/septic properties carry maintenance costs that municipal buyers don't face. Verify the exact tax figure on the specific parcel.

Property taxes. Budget roughly 1.6–1.8% of assessed value annually as a planning range, and confirm the exact number per parcel — it shifts with the school district (Waunakee vs. Middleton-Cross Plains) and the town, county, and technical-college portions. Wisconsin is a high-property-tax state by national standards; buyers from no-income-tax states are routinely surprised by the full bill.

The waterfront premium. True Lake Mendota shoreline is scarce and priced accordingly — it's the top of the Westport market and rarely trades. Near-shore and rural-residential parcels offer the country setting at more accessible price points.

Veteran relief. Veterans rated 100% permanent and total disabled may eliminate the property tax burden on their primary residence entirely through the WDVA credit — a meaningful factor in a high-tax county.

11 — The Honest Buyer's Guide

What Agents Don't Always Lead With

No spin. The six things that actually matter once you're making an offer on a Westport home.

01 — Verify the school district by parcel. Two top districts serve one town, and the line doesn't follow mailing addresses. The assigned schools — and the mill rate — can change from one lot to the next. This is the #1 Westport due-diligence item.

02 — Assume well and septic until proven otherwise. Many parcels aren't on municipal water and sewer. Budget for water testing, septic inspection, and long-term maintenance. It's normal for rural living — but it's a real cost and a real responsibility.

03 — Understand shoreland zoning before you buy for the lake. If water access is the whole point, county shoreland rules govern what you can build, add, or remove near the shore. Know them before you write, not after.

04 — The incorporation vote is live. Westport is mid-process on becoming a village. It isn't decided. Near the town's edges, the proposed boundary matters — ask for the current status and how it affects the specific parcel.

05 — There's no downtown — by design. If you want to walk to coffee and a fish fry, Westport isn't that; Waunakee and Middleton are ten minutes away. Make sure the trade of convenience for space is the trade you actually want.

06 — Inventory is thin and setting-specific. True waterfront and acreage don't come up often and move fast when they do. Working with an agent who watches this micro-market — including homes not yet listed — is how buyers get in.

12 — FAQ

Common Questions, Specific Answers

The questions buyers actually ask when researching a move to Westport, answered as cleanly as possible.

Is Westport a good place to live?

Yes, for the right buyer. It offers larger lots and a rural, lakeside setting on Lake Mendota's north shore, access to top-rated Waunakee schools (with a portion in Middleton-Cross Plains), Governor Nelson State Park, and a ten-minute commute to Madison. The trade-off is limited commercial services of its own — shopping, dining, and civic life happen in neighboring Waunakee, Middleton, and Madison.

What school district serves Westport?

Westport is served by two districts depending on where a parcel sits: the Waunakee Community School District (most of the town — #7 in Wisconsin overall) and the Middleton-Cross Plains Area School District, also top-rated. Because the town straddles two districts, always verify the assigned schools at the specific parcel before writing an offer — the line does not follow ZIP codes or mailing addresses. The Town's own incorporation FAQ confirms these two districts, and notes the assignment would not change if Westport becomes a village.

Is Westport becoming a village?

It's in process, not decided. The Town Board voted unanimously in September 2024 to support incorporation and filed the petition on October 4, 2024. A judge found it met criteria in December 2024, the town filed with the state Incorporation Review Board in 2025, and surrounding communities approved a cooperative boundary agreement in early 2026. If it advances through the state board, residents would vote in a referendum, hoped for late 2026 or spring 2027. Confirm the current status before relying on it.

Why is Westport trying to incorporate?

Westport is an "island" town surrounded by Madison, Middleton, Waunakee, and DeForest, which can control land use near their borders and have annexed pieces of it for decades. Incorporating as a village would stop further annexation, fix its borders, keep local control of growth and land use, and protect the Lake Mendota watershed and the town's rural character.

What is Westport known for?

Lake Mendota's quieter north shore, Governor Nelson State Park with its sandy beach and Capitol views, larger rural and waterfront lots, and a deliberate focus on preserving rural character. It was named by Irish settlers after Westport in County Mayo, Ireland — and the Village of Waunakee was originally carved out of the Town of Westport in 1893.

How far is Westport from Madison?

Westport sits immediately north of Madison on the Lake Mendota shoreline. Downtown Madison is roughly 10 miles south via Highway 113 or the Highway 19 corridor — about 15 to 20 minutes outside rush hour. Dane County Regional Airport is about 5 miles away.

Does Westport have its own schools, downtown, or restaurants?

No. Westport is a low-density town without its own downtown, school buildings, or commercial core. Residents use neighboring Waunakee, Middleton, and Madison for schools, shopping, dining, healthcare, and civic events. That's by design — the town prioritizes rural and lakeside character over commercial density.
